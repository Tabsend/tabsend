# Tabsend Contributor Licence Agreement

> **This is a non-binding draft.** It was written by the project owner, who is not a lawyer,
> to be short and readable rather than exhaustive. A lawyer should review it before this
> project relies on it. If you are contributing on behalf of an employer, check with them
> first — in most places, code you write on your employer's time or equipment belongs to them,
> not to you, and only they can grant these rights.

By agreeing to this document you give the owner of Tabsend permission to use what you
contribute. It does not take your copyright away, and it does not stop you doing whatever you
like with your own work elsewhere.

## 1. What this covers

A **contribution** is anything you deliberately submit to the project — code, documentation,
tests, configuration — by pull request, patch, or any other route. It does not cover things
you clearly mark as "not a contribution" when you submit them.

## 2. Copyright licence

You keep the copyright in your contribution. You grant the owner a perpetual, worldwide,
irrevocable, royalty-free, non-exclusive licence to use, copy, modify, distribute, publicly
display, and create derivative works from your contribution, **and to sublicense and
relicense it on any terms**, including in a commercial or closed-source product.

The relicensing right is the point of this agreement. It is what lets Tabsend ship a hosted
commercial version, and lets its licensing change in future without tracking down every past
contributor for permission. Without it, that becomes permanently impossible.

## 3. Patent licence

If your contribution is covered by any patent you own or control, you grant the owner and
everyone who receives the software a perpetual, worldwide, irrevocable, royalty-free,
non-exclusive licence under those patent claims to make, use, sell, offer to sell, import, and
otherwise transfer the work. This mirrors the patent grant in Apache-2.0 §3.

## 4. What you are promising

You confirm that:

- You wrote the contribution yourself, or you otherwise have the right to submit it under
  these terms.
- If your employer has rights to your work, they have given permission — or you have their
  written waiver.
- Any third-party code, assets, or dependencies inside your contribution are clearly
  identified, along with their licences and any restrictions you know about.

## 5. What you keep

Everything else. Your copyright stays yours. You can use, publish, sell, or relicense your own
contribution however you want, anywhere else, including in a project that competes with
Tabsend. This licence is non-exclusive and takes nothing away from you.

## 6. No obligation

The owner is under no obligation to use, merge, keep, or ship your contribution, and may
remove or replace it at any time for any reason.

## 7. No warranty

You provide your contribution "as is", without warranties of any kind, to the extent the law
allows. You are not expected to provide support for it, and you take on no liability for it.

## 8. If something turns out to be wrong

If you later discover that any of the promises in section 4 was inaccurate — for example, you
find that code you submitted wasn't yours to give — please tell the owner by opening an
issue on the project's GitHub page as soon as you can.

## How to agree

Put this line in your pull request description or as a comment on the pull request:

```
I have read the Tabsend CLA (CLA.md) and I agree to it for this and all my future
contributions to this project.
```

You only need to do this once. See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) for why this mechanism
was chosen over a CLA bot or a `Signed-off-by` line.
